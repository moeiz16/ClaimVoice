# Size check fact sheet

*How big is this claim likely to be?* Decides fast-track or adjuster.

## What it is for

- Estimates how likely a new claim is to be large or complex, so simple claims can be fast-tracked and the rest go straight to an adjuster.
- Runs once, when the call ends. A person can change the route with a reason at any time.

## What it is not for

- Deciding whether a claim is covered or how much is paid.
- Pricing a policy, or judging the caller.

## What it looks at

- A collision
- Age of the vehicle
- Airbags went off
- Another party involved
- Auto or home policy
- Caller's own estimate
- Coverage limit
- Deductible
- Fire or smoke
- Hail
- Home can't be lived in
- Home open to the weather
- How many parts are damaged
- How much the caller described
- Matches an active storm
- Photos coming
- Police report
- Roof, window or skylight damage
- Rooms affected
- Siding or fence damage
- Someone hurt
- Theft or break-in
- Time from loss to call
- Vehicle can't be driven
- Water
- Water still coming in
- Weather records confirm it
- Wind

## What it never looks at

- No voice stress, accent, speech-rate or pitch features: unreliable and discriminatory.
- Proxy for protected characteristics; region-level effects are tested for, not learned.
- Age is used only to form age bands for fairness testing.
- Identity is not evidence.
- The caller's language is tested for fairness, never used to route.
- Where the caller lives is tested for fairness, never used to route.

## How it was tested

- On 0.89 AUC terms it separates large from small claims well; at the setting in use it sends 91 in 100 of large or complex claims to an adjuster.
- Its scores are calibrated: when it says 30 in 100, about 30 in 100 such claims turn out large (expected calibration error 0.012).
- It beats a simple scorecard on the same data (PR-AUC 0.77 against 0.72).

## Fairness

- The fast-track rate is 40 in 100 overall. Every region, language and age band stays within 5.0 points of it (largest gap 3.5 points, Québec).
- Age, language and region are never used to route a claim. We use them only here, to check fairness.

## Limits

- It learned from synthetic claim calls shaped like the ones the line takes, with real claim costs from French motor insurance data (freMTPL2). Its absolute accuracy describes that data, not a real book of business; what carries over is the method: calibration, reasons, fairness tests, monitoring and sign-off.
- It only knows what the caller said and what the policy shows. Photos and an adjuster's visit come later.

## Sign-offs

- A version goes into use only when every gate passes and two different people sign: a claims supervisor and compliance (model risk).

## For the technical reviewer

- Algorithm: Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost), tuned with Optuna; isotonic calibration on the validation months.
- Split: Time split: trained on losses before 1 July 2025, calibrated on July to September, tested on October to December 2025.
- Test: AUC 0.887, PR-AUC 0.766, Brier 0.0921, ECE 0.0119.
- Logistic baseline: AUC 0.849, PR-AUC 0.720; random forest: AUC 0.870, PR-AUC 0.750.
- Before calibration: Brier 0.0916, ECE 0.0178; Platt scaling: Brier 0.0918, ECE 0.0168.
- Reasons: exact TreeSHAP values; top-5 overlap across 5 bootstrap refits 1.00 (Jaccard).
- Public benchmark on freMTPL2 alone (top tenth of claim cost from policy fields): logistic AUC 0.61, XGBoost AUC 0.62. Real French motor claims, policy fields only (no driver age, no region). Whether a claim lands in the top tenth of cost is only weakly predictable from the policy alone; the size of a loss depends on what happened, which is why the size check is trained on what the caller tells the line.
