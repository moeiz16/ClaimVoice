# Closer-look check fact sheet

*Should someone in special investigations look at it?* Asks special investigations to look. Never denies.

## What it is for

- Asks someone in special investigations to look at a claim when several documented signs line up.
- A referral is a request for review. It is not a finding of fraud and never denies a claim.

## What it is not for

- Denying, delaying or reducing a claim.
- Judging a person by who they are: it never sees age, language, region or anything about their voice.

## What it looks at

- A collision
- Auto or home policy
- Caller's own estimate
- Changes to when, where or what happened
- Claims in the last 3 years
- Fire or smoke
- Night-time loss with no witness or report
- Photos declined
- Police report
- Policy not in force on the loss date
- Round-number estimate in the top band
- Someone else pushing for a fast payment
- Theft not reported to police
- Theft or break-in
- Time from loss to call
- Time since coverage last changed
- Time since the policy started
- Water
- Weather records don't show the storm
- Wind

## What it never looks at

- No voice stress, accent, speech-rate or pitch features: unreliable and discriminatory.
- Proxy for protected characteristics; region-level effects are tested for, not learned.
- Age is used only to form age bands for fairness testing.
- Identity is not evidence.
- The caller's language is tested for fairness, never used to route.
- Where the caller lives is tested for fairness, never used to route.

## How it was tested

- It refers 4 in 100 claims for review and needs at least two signs before it can refer one.
- Of the claims it refers, 48 in 100 turn out to need a closer look in testing; it finds 43 in 100 of those that do.
- It beats a simple scorecard on the same data (PR-AUC 0.39 against 0.22); scores are calibrated (expected calibration error 0.007).

## Fairness

- The closer-look rate is 4 in 100 overall. Every region, language and age band stays within 1.5 points of it (largest gap 0.3 points, 65 and over).
- Age, language and region are never used to route a claim. We use them only here, to check fairness.

## Limits

- It learned from synthetic claim calls shaped like the ones the line takes, with real claim costs from French motor insurance data (freMTPL2). Its absolute accuracy describes that data, not a real book of business; what carries over is the method: calibration, reasons, fairness tests, monitoring and sign-off.
- It only knows what the caller said and what the policy shows. Photos and an adjuster's visit come later.
- The closer-look labels come from documented patterns injected into the synthetic data, with 3 in 100 labels flipped to mimic investigators disagreeing.

## Sign-offs

- A version goes into use only when every gate passes and two different people sign: a claims supervisor and compliance (model risk).

## For the technical reviewer

- Algorithm: Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost), tuned with Optuna; isotonic calibration on the validation months.
- Split: Time split: trained on losses before 1 July 2025, calibrated on July to September, tested on October to December 2025.
- Test: AUC 0.857, PR-AUC 0.389, Brier 0.0293, ECE 0.0072.
- Logistic baseline: AUC 0.806, PR-AUC 0.224; random forest: AUC 0.856, PR-AUC 0.222.
- Before calibration: Brier 0.0562, ECE 0.1216; Platt scaling: Brier 0.0289, ECE 0.0051.
- Reasons: exact TreeSHAP values; top-5 overlap across 5 bootstrap refits 0.80 (Jaccard).
