# Data

Everything ClaimVoice uses is public or synthetic. Downloaded data lives in the git-ignored `data/` folder and is
never committed. Nothing about a real person is used anywhere.

| Source | What for | Size | Licence | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| freMTPL2freq (OpenML 41214) | real motor policies: exposure, vehicle, driver and area features | 678,013 rows; 7.5 MB Parquet | Listed as CC0 on OpenML; originally from the CASdatasets R package (GPL-2+) by Dutang and Charpentier | `make data` → `data/fremtpl2/` |
| freMTPL2sev (OpenML 41215) | real claim amounts for those policies | 26,639 rows; 0.3 MB Parquet | as above | `make data` → `data/fremtpl2/` |
| Synthetic intake records | training and testing the routing checks | 18,000 calls (+ a 20,000-call fairness audit sample and 8 recent weeks), generated in memory with fixed seeds | MIT (this repository) | `backend/claimvoice/ml/synth.py` (`make train`, `make eval`) |
| Larkspur Mutual seed | the demo organisation, policies, calls and claims | ~200 policies, ~70 calls | MIT (this repository) | generated by `make seed` into the database |
| Scripted test callers | dialogue evaluation and the live demo lines | 3+ YAML files, EN/FR | MIT | `eval/callers/` |
| Weather alert snapshot | confirming storm losses and the surge banner | 3 alerts | adapted from public Environment and Climate Change Canada and US National Weather Service alert formats; no alert text is copied verbatim | `backend/claimvoice/tools/data/weather_snapshot.json` |
| Landing-page sample call audio | the recorded call on the landing page, EN and FR | 1.1 MB total (Opus + AAC + timed captions) | generated once with macOS `say` system voices (synthetic voices, labelled "Sample call, voices are synthetic") | `frontend/public/audio/dana-call*` |

## freMTPL2

French motor third-party liability data published with *Computational Actuarial Science with R* (Charpentier, ed.,
2014) and distributed in CASdatasets; OpenML hosts a copy (`freMTPL2freq`, `freMTPL2sev`), verified reachable as
Parquet and listed under CC0. Citation: C. Dutang and A. Charpentier (2018), *CASdatasets: Insurance datasets*. We
download it at setup (`scripts/fetch_data.py`, checksum-pinned in `scripts/data_pins.json`) and never redistribute
it.

Known properties, checked by `scripts/smoke/ml_smoke.py`:
- OpenML's freq table has 678,013 rows (the package description says 677,991; a known duplicate-ID quirk), so we key
  on `IDpol` and deduplicate.
- 26,639 claim amounts join to 26,444 policies (195 claim rows have no matching policy and are dropped).
- Claim cost is heavy-tailed: the top decile carries most of the total.
- From policy features alone, "large claim" (top decile of cost) is only weakly predictable (logistic AUC ≈ 0.61,
  XGBoost ≈ 0.63 in the smoke run). That is expected: the size of a loss depends mostly on what happened, which a
  policy table cannot see. It is why the size check is trained on intake facts (below) and uses freMTPL2 for the
  realistic shape of claim costs.

Use in ClaimVoice:
1. **Cost distributions.** Synthetic intake records draw their final claim cost from freMTPL2's empirical severity
   distribution, conditioned on vehicle age and power bands for motor losses, and rescaled by loss type for
   property losses, so the "large or complex" label has a realistic tail.
2. **Public benchmark.** The eval report includes a freMTPL2-only large-claim benchmark (baseline vs XGBoost on real
   data) so the method is shown on public data, not only on data we generated. `DrivAge` is never a feature; it
   forms age bands for the fairness check only.

## Synthetic intake records (training the routing checks)

`ml/synth.py` generates claim calls that look like what the line collects: loss type, timing, safety answers,
damage facts, police report, photos, caller estimate band, policy tenure and coverage changes, prior claims, weather
match and the call's corrections. Each record is a claim file with the same field keys the line fills, the policy
facts the platform holds and the call facts the engine reports, turned into model inputs by the same
`build_features` the live scorer uses (no training/serving skew). Records span January 2023 to December 2025 by loss
month, with more calls in winter and a growing book; the split is by time (train before July 2025, calibrate and
tune on July–September, test on October–December 2025) so no future pattern leaks into training. The loss mix differs
by season and region (wind and water in winter, hail in summer, more water in Québec), with theft and collision shares
the same everywhere.

**Claim cost and the size check's label.** Each claim's cost is drawn from freMTPL2's real claim amounts (by
vehicle-age and power band), scaled by loss type (fire 3.6×, water 2×, wind 1.5×, hail 1.3×, theft 1.1×) and moved by
the facts that make a loss bigger: an injury (2.8×), a vehicle that can't be driven (1.9×), airbags (1.8×), a home open
to the weather (2.3×), water still coming in (1.7×), each extra room (+35 %), a home that can't be lived in (2.2×) and
each extra damaged part (+22 %), with lognormal noise. "Large or complex" (about 22 % of calls) is the top fifth of
cost within the line, or an injury with another party involved, or a home that can't be lived in. A hidden "severe"
draw (12 % of calls) raises several facts together, as real bad losses do.

**Injected fraud patterns** (documented so the closer-look check is evaluated against known truth), each injected
independently at a stated rate, with a weight in a latent score so no single sign is decisive:

| Pattern | How it is injected | Rate | Weight |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| New policy | loss within 30 days of the policy start | 5 % | 2.70 |
| Coverage raised before the loss | coverage or limit raised within 45 days before the loss | 4 % | 2.29 |
| Late report | reported more than 14 days after the loss (more again after 30 days) | 5 % | 1.76 |
| Story changes | two or more corrections to date, place or sequence during the call | 4 % | 2.57 |
| Weather contradiction | wind or hail damage where the weather snapshot has no event | 12 % of storm losses | 2.84 |
| Theft without a police report | theft with no police report | 20 % of thefts | 2.16 |
| Round-number estimate | caller's estimate is a round figure in the top band | 3 % | 1.62 |
| Third-party pressure | someone other than the policyholder pushing for a fast payment | 2.5 % | 3.24 |
| Prior claims cluster | three or more claims in 36 months | 3 % | 1.76 |
| Photos declined | the caller declines to send photos | 6 % | 1.08 |

Some signs compound when they appear together (new policy with raised coverage, story changes with third-party
pressure, a late report with a theft and no police report or with declined photos), which is where a tree model can
beat an additive scorecard. The latent score starts at −5.3 (about 1 in 200 with no sign); about 3.7 % of records are
labelled "worth a closer look" (fraud or material misrepresentation found on review), and 3 % of labels are redrawn at
random to mimic investigators disagreeing. Region, language and age band are generated with realistic
mixes and stored only for fairness testing; patterns are injected independently of them, so any gap the model shows
is the model's.

**The active surge.** "Chinook windstorm, Calgary": Environment and Climate Change Canada-style wind warning (gusts
100–120 km/h) from 8 p.m. the evening before; calls 3.4× usual since 6 a.m. (computed live: 9 Alberta calls since 6

**Why wind, not hail.** Calgary's hail season is June to August; a hail surge in January would not be credible.
Chinook windstorms with gusts over 100 km/h are typical Calgary winter events and damage roofs, siding, fences and
skylights, so the demo stays true to the season. Hail remains a supported loss type with its own script.

## Retention of demo data

`make reset` deletes the local database and recreates the seed. Recordings and photos from local test calls live in
`data/recordings` and `data/uploads` (git-ignored). Public demo calls from the landing page are never kept after they
end.
